Game concept · survival · single player

The Sheep Are Angry!

The humans are gone. The fences still stand, the feed sheds are still full, and nobody is coming to shear you. You are one ewe with a yellow ear tag. The sheep intend to inherit the Earth.

Tag01

Premise

A catastrophe has emptied the world of people overnight. Farms are silent, gates are latched, and five thousand years of domestication have left sheep badly suited to fending for themselves. Every other species smells opportunity: feral dogs, returning wolves, foxes, crows, and above all the goats, who think they should be in charge.

You play Bramble, a ewe who gets out through a gap in the wire on the first morning. Her goal starts as staying alive until nightfall. It ends with the flock running the valley, then the country.

Tag02

Core loop

Each in-game day is about four real minutes. Days are calm and nights are dangerous.

  • Graze
  • →
  • Find strays
  • →
  • Grow the flock
  • →
  • Survive the night
  • →
  • Claim pasture

Grazing fills hunger, but grass you strip takes days to regrow, so the flock has to keep moving. Strays you find join and follow you with real flocking behaviour. A bigger flock is safer at night but eats a pasture bare faster, which is the tension that drives you outward into new ground.

Tag03

The fleece problem

Modern sheep never stop growing wool, and without shearing it becomes a hazard. This is the mechanic that makes the game about sheep rather than any animal.

Weight
Fleece grows every day. Past 4 kg you slow down; past 8 kg you can't outrun a fox.
Heat
In summer a heavy fleece causes heat stress. Shade and water matter.
Getting cast
A heavy sheep that rolls onto its back can't get up. Flockmates have to nudge it upright before the crows arrive.
Shedding
Rub against fence posts, rocks and barbed wire to tear fleece free. Shed wool becomes the flock's building material.
Winter
The trade-off flips. Thick fleece keeps you alive in snow, so you want to arrive at the cold season heavy.
Tag04

Threats

Predators

Foxes take lambs at dawn. Feral sheepdogs hunt in packs and still remember how to split a flock. Wolves arrive in the second year.

The land

Bogs, cliff edges, flash floods, blizzards and drought. Fields eaten bare. Water troughs that no one refills.

The body

Hunger, thirst, heat, flystrike and getting cast. Sickness spreads through a crowded flock.

Rivals

Goats climb better, eat anything and want the same valley. Feral pigs tear up pasture. Crows watch for weakness.

Tag05

Rising to dominance

Sheep win through numbers and cooperation. Bramble never becomes a fighter; the flock becomes an organism.

The ring
Flockmates bunched around you form a defensive ring. Predators that meet a tight ring of five or more give up.
Rams
Recruited rams can charge. Aim them like a cannon at whatever threatens the ewes.
Old knowledge
The flock learns to use what humans left: headbutt gate latches open, raid feed sheds, lure wolves into electric fencing that still runs on solar panels.
Turning the dogs
Lost border collies can be won over. A dog that once herded sheep now herds wolves away from them.
The Commons
Claimed pastures become territory on the map. Shed wool builds windbreaks, nests for lambing and, later, markers that warn goats off.
Tag06

Progression

Progress is measured in flock size and in the land the flock holds. Each rank unlocks new behaviour, and the seasons act as chapters.

  1. 1sheep

    Stray

    Just Bramble. Learn to graze, hide in hedges and shed fleece. Survive the first night.

  2. 5+sheep

    Flock

    The ring unlocks. Strays follow. Spring brings the first lambs.

  3. 20+sheep

    Herd

    Rams charge on command. Gates and feed sheds open. A sentinel keeps watch at night.

  4. 60+sheep

    Commons

    Claim valleys, win collies to your side, face the goats for the high pasture.

  5. 200+sheep

    Dominion

    Several flocks under one ear tag. Pick a direction: rebuild the farms, or let the fields go wild.

Losing Bramble ends the run, but a lamb with her tag can take over if one is old enough. That lets a long game survive a bad night.

Tag07

Look and feel

Played top down, with Bramble always in the centre of the screen and the world scrolling around her. A small map in the corner shows the flock, nearby strays and anything hunting them. The art is hand-drawn pastoral countryside slowly going wild: hedgerows thickening, tractors rusting, roads cracking under grass. The tone is warm and a little absurd, with real stakes. Sheep bleat to each other in short subtitled lines. Nights are lit only by the moon and your flock's pale wool.

Draft 1, for the Sheep Survival project. A playable prototype of the first night accompanies this page.
